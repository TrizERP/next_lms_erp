Overview

Each section below rubrics one part of the chapter across four bands – Excellent, Good, Fair, Needs Improvement – so a mark reflects understanding, not just a right-or-wrong answer. Each section closes with sample questions that are new to this document, chosen so that, combined with the Teacher Training deck's two banks (9 questions, then 13 more), you have over 35 real, non-repeating questions across the chapter.

Sample questions
  • Assertion-Reason (2 marks): Assertion – the expression 4x + 5y + 3 has three terms. Reason – terms are separated by + or − signs.
  • MCQ (1 mark): Which of the following is a univariate polynomial? (a) 3x + 2y (b) 4x² + 5 (c) 2x + 3y − z (d) x² + y²
  • Short Answer (2 marks): Determine the degree of 4y² + 3y − 1 and classify it.

Sample questions
  • MCQ (1 mark): Which equation represents a linear relationship between x and y? (a) y = x² + 3 (b) y = 2x + 5 (c) y = 1/x (d) y = 3x
  • Assertion-Reason (1 mark): Assertion – the equation y = 5 − 3x is a linear equation. Reason – it can be expressed as y = ax + b, with a = −3 and b = 5.

Putting It Together

Notes for teachers
  • Vocabulary across the chapter: term, coefficient, constant, degree, linear polynomial, slope, y-intercept, parallel, coincident
  • Activities: card-sorting by degree, perimeter discovery for ax, tile-pattern nth-term derivation, telecom-billing table discovery, graph-paper plotting of parallel-slope lines
  • Bloom's verbs used throughout: identify, classify, derive, model, compare, graph, justify, evaluate
  • Evidence types: labelled expressions, derived nth terms, worked graphs, written justifications, error-analysis of a wrong parallel-lines claim
