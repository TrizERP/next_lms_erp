Learning Outcomes

Introduction to Linear Polynomials. 10 sections, matching the Teacher Training deck, drawn from the chapter's own curriculum competencies (C-2.1, C-3.2, C-8.1) and its per-concept learning objectives.

Section 1

By the End of This Chapter

Every outcome in this document is measurable and assessment-ready – a teacher can watch a student do each one and judge, without ambiguity, whether it has been met.

  • •Identify the term, variable, coefficient and constant in an algebraic expression such as 4x + 5y + 3.
  • •Determine whether a polynomial is univariate, and state its degree from the highest exponent present.
  • •Test whether a given expression is a linear polynomial, using the degree-1 and non-negative-integer-exponent rules.
  • •Recognise a linear pattern from a constant difference, and derive its nth term.
  • •Model a real situation of constant increase or constant decrease as linear growth or linear decay.
  • •Write a real scenario in the form y = ax + b, and identify the slope a and the y-intercept b.
  • •Compare the steepness of two lines from their slopes, and find a line's y-intercept from its equation or from two points.
  • •Graph a linear equation using the two-point method, and determine whether two given lines are parallel.
Section 2 · C-2.1

Expressions, Univariate Polynomials, Degree

Competency C-2.1 (understanding of powers and exponents) anchors the degree and classification work in this section; Algebraic Expressions itself is prerequisite review with no curriculum code of its own, so its outcomes are drawn directly from the chapter's own learning objectives.

  • C-2.1-LO-1Identifies the degree of a univariate polynomial from the highest power of its variable, and names a polynomial of degree one as linear.
  • C-2.1-LO-2Distinguishes a polynomial from an algebraic expression that is not one, by checking that every exponent of the variable is a whole number.
  • LOIdentifies terms, variables, coefficients and constants in an algebraic expression such as 4x + 5y + 3.
  • LOModels a real-life situation, such as buying items in two sizes of box, by constructing an algebraic expression.
  • LOCompares two algebraic expressions to determine similarities and differences in their terms and coefficients.
PrerequisitesVariables, exponents and basic arithmetic operations from earlier grades. If the degree-vs-term-count confusion persists, Slide 2 of the Teacher Training deck's card-sorting activity re-establishes it quickly.
Section 3 · C-2.1

Linear Polynomial

The outcome that the rest of the chapter is built on: everything from here on is a linear polynomial wearing a different real-world costume.

  • C-2.1-LO-1Names a polynomial of degree one as linear, and gives examples both with and without a constant term.
  • LOModels a real-world situation with a constant rate of change, such as a fence's perimeter or a membership fee, as a linear polynomial.
  • LOPredicts the change in the output of a linear polynomial when its input changes by a constant amount.
PrerequisitesSection 2's degree test, and comfort writing an algebraic expression from a word problem.
Section 4 · C-3.2

Linear Pattern

Competency C-3.2 begins here: modelling a contextualised situation and drawing a conclusion from it, starting with the chapter's own growing-tile pattern.

  • C-3.2-LO-1Recognises a linear pattern in a table of values by testing whether the differences between successive terms are constant.
  • C-3.2-LO-2Writes the rule of a linear pattern in the form ax + b, and uses it to predict a term far along the pattern.
  • LOAnalyses a linear pattern to derive its general term from given terms or visual stages, such as the tile pattern's 2n − 1.
PrerequisitesSection 3's linear-polynomial form, and basic sequence vocabulary (term, position).
Section 5 · C-3.2

Linear Growth and Linear Decay

  • C-3.2-LO-3Models situations of linear growth and of linear decay, such as a rising cost or a falling tank level, with a linear polynomial.
  • LODifferentiates linear growth from exponential growth, and linear decay from exponential decay, by checking for constant addition versus constant multiplication.
  • LOApplies a linear growth or decay model to predict a future value, such as a cost at a distance not yet in the table.
PrerequisitesSection 4's constant-difference test, applied now to real, signed contexts (increasing cost, decreasing height).
Section 6 · C-3.2, C-8.1

Linear Relationship, y = ax + b

The point where C-3.2's modelling work and C-8.1's graphing work meet: the same equation, read two ways.

  • C-3.2-LO-2Applies the linear relationship formula y = ax + b to describe a growing pattern or a billed cost.
  • C-8.1-LO-4Draws the graph of a linear relationship from a table of values, and states why the points lie on a straight line.
  • LOAnalyses a set of tabulated data, such as a telecom bill at several usage levels, to derive the linear equation y = ax + b.
  • LOCreates a novel real-world situation, distinct from the chapter's own examples, that can be modelled by a linear equation.
Section 7 · C-8.1

Slope of a Line

  • C-8.1-LO-5Reads the slope from the graph or equation of a linear relationship, and interprets it in the situation being modelled.
  • LOCompares the steepness of two lines of the form y = ax by analysing their slopes.
  • LOPredicts the relative steepness of y = ax compared with y = x, for any given value of a.
PrerequisitesSection 6's y = ax + b form, and comfort comparing signed numbers by magnitude.
Section 8 · C-8.1

Y-Intercept

  • C-8.1-LO-5Reads the y-intercept from the graph or equation of a linear relationship, and interprets it in the situation being modelled.
  • LOApplies the rule (set x = 0) to determine the y-intercept from a variety of linear equations.
  • LOAnalyses how changing the y-intercept value affects a linear equation's graph and its real-world interpretation.
PrerequisitesSection 6's y = ax + b form; this outcome and Section 7's are usually assessed together, since both read different parts of the same equation.
Section 9 · C-8.1

Graphing Linear Equations and Parallel Lines

  • C-8.1-LO-4Draws the graph of a linear relationship using the two-point method, and states why the points lie on a straight line.
  • C-8.1-LO-6Explains why two linear relationships with the same slope give parallel lines, and predicts the effect of changing the intercept.
  • LOEvaluates the graphical behaviour (rising or falling) of a line directly from the sign of its slope.
  • LOUses the concept of parallel lines to find the equation of a line parallel to a given line, passing through a stated point.

Section 10

Closing

Competency C-2.1 (understanding powers and exponents) carries the chapter's opening classification work – expressions, univariate polynomials, degree, and the definition of "linear" itself. Competency C-3.2 (modelling contextualised problems with equations) carries the pattern, growth and decay outcomes. Competency C-8.1 (daily-life phenomena via graphs, tables and equations) carries everything downstream of y = ax + b: slope, y-intercept, graphing and parallel lines.

One further competency code, C-3.1 (remainder theorem, factor theorem and division algorithm), is attached to this chapter in the curriculum table but carries no recorded outcomes here and describes a later polynomial-division topic, not this introductory chapter – it is deliberately excluded from this document rather than stretched to fit.

Together, these three competencies and the twenty-six outcomes listed across Sections 1–9 cover everything this document claims – none were invented for this write-up, and none duplicate another section's wording. A student who can demonstrate every outcome above has, in a concrete and checkable sense, finished the chapter.
