Section 1

Overview

Nine concepts in two moves: first the plane itself (the coordinate system, points on axes, coordinates and quadrants), then the three formulas built on top of it (distance, reflection, midpoint). Every row in this document traces to a specific line in the chapter – the floor-grid story, Fig. 1.4's points Q(−5, 3) and S(3, −5), and triangle ADM with A(3, 4) and D(7, 1) – and every teaching strategy is one the chapter's own concept data recommends, not a generic suggestion.

The Distance Formula

KnowledgeSkillAbilityLevel

Triangle ADM: horizontal leg 4, vertical leg 3, hypotenuse 5Construct a right triangle from two points to find distanceGeometric reasoningIntermediate

Recommended strategy – inquiry-based teachingGuide students to derive the formula themselves from the Pythagorean theorem rather than presenting it as a given – this builds genuine ownership of where the formula comes from.
Where this shows upThe shortest walking route between two points on a city map. A GPS calculating straight-line distance. An architect measuring between two points on a blueprint. An environmental scientist measuring between sampling sites.

Reflection

KnowledgeSkillAbilityLevel
Reflecting (x,y) in the y-axis gives (−x,y)Apply the rule to find a reflected point's coordinatesProcedural fluencyBasic
Reflection preserves distance between pointsVerify preserved length using the distance formulaVerification reasoningIntermediate
Reflection is a rigid transformation (an isometry)Explain why a reflected shape is congruent to the originalConceptual reasoningAdvanced
Recommended strategy – activity-based teachingGraph-paper folding lets students physically experience a reflection and discover the coordinate rule for themselves, rather than being told which coordinate changes.
Where this shows upA bathroom mirror's lateral inversion. Computer graphics software flipping an image. Symmetry in nature – a butterfly's wings, a leaf's venation. Architectural symmetry, as in the Taj Mahal's facade.

The Midpoint Formula

Recommended strategy – scenario-based teachingFrame the formula around a real scenario – two friends agreeing to meet exactly halfway – before naming it, since this is what the chapter's own S/M/T discovery table is already doing.
Where this shows upA fair halfway meeting point between two locations on a map. A construction team centring a steel beam to balance a load. Computer graphics software centring an object between two reference points.
