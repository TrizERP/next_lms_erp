Overview

Each section below rubrics one part of the chapter across four bands – Excellent, Good, Fair, Needs Improvement – so a mark reflects understanding, not just a right-or-wrong answer. Each section also closes with a short set of sample questions, drawn from the chapter's own assessment bank, that you can hand out directly or adapt.

Section 2

The Coordinate System

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
Axis & Origin IDIdentifies axes and origin instantly; explains why two perpendicular lines are neededCorrect with brief explanationIdentifies but can't explain purposeConfuses x-axis and y-axis
Sign ConventionPlaces a point from any stated direction, including mixedCorrect for single-directionCorrect only when both positiveCan't reliably apply it
Sample questions
  • MCQ (1 mark): Which of the following correctly identifies the horizontal and vertical lines? (a) x-axis is vertical, y-axis horizontal (b) x-axis horizontal, y-axis vertical (c) both horizontal (d) both vertical
  • Assertion-Reason (1 mark): Assertion – the origin is where the x-axis and y-axis cross. Reason – its coordinates are (0,0). Are both true, and does the reason correctly explain the assertion?
  • Numerical (1 mark): A point is 3 units away from the origin along the positive x-axis. What are its coordinates?
Section 3

Points on Axes

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
Axis-Point ClassificationStates (x,0)/(0,y) and identifies the axis from the zero coordinateClassifies most axis points correctlyCorrect only when zero is firstCan't distinguish an axis point
Sample questions
  • MCQ (1 mark): Which of the following points lies on the x-axis, other than the origin? (a) (0,3) (b) (3,0) (c) (3,2) (d) (0,0)
  • Short Answer (2 marks): Given the point P (−4, 0): (a) on which axis does it lie? (b) describe its position relative to the origin.
  • HOTS (4 marks): A student says, "The point (−5, 0) lies to the right of the origin because 5 is larger than 0." Evaluate the reasoning, identify the error, and give the correct explanation.

The Distance Formula

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement

ApplicationCorrect for any pair, including negativesCorrect for positives onlyFrequently drops square/rootCan't apply correctly
Sample questions
  • Numerical (3 marks): Find the distance between the points A (−2, 5) and B (4, −3).
  • Short Answer (2 marks): Using the figure of a right triangle formed by two points, derive the distance formula from the Pythagorean theorem.
  • HOTS (4 marks): Using the distance formula, show that the points (0, 0), (10, 0) and (5, 5√3) form an equilateral triangle.

Reflection

Sample questions
  • MCQ (1 mark): What is the image of the point (3, 4) when reflected across the y-axis?
  • Short Answer (2 marks): Reflect the point P (−2, 5) across the y-axis, then reflect Q (4, −3) across the y-axis. Write both new coordinates.
  • Long Answer (4 marks): Given A (2, 3) and B (−4, 5), find the coordinates of their reflections A′ and B′ across the y-axis. Using the distance formula, prove that AB = A′B′.
Section 8

The Midpoint Formula

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
ApplicationCorrect for any two points, including negativesCorrect for positives onlyCorrect only on the same axisCan't apply the formula
Missing-EndpointSolves algebraically in any quadrantMinor arithmetic slipsOnly with step-by-step guidanceCan't work backwards
Sample questions
  • MCQ (1 mark): What is the midpoint of the segment joining (2, 3) and (4, 7)?
  • Assertion-Reason (2 marks): Assertion – for S (−8, 7) and T (6, −3), the midpoint is (0, −2). Reason – the midpoint averages the x- and y-coordinates of the endpoints.
  • Long Answer (4 marks): The points S (−8, 7), M (0, −2) and T (6, −3) are given. Determine whether M is the midpoint of ST, showing all work. If not, find the actual midpoint.
