Linear Equations in One Variable. 10 sections, matching the Teacher Training deck, grounded in the chapter's own worked examples and its concept-by-concept pedagogy data.

Section 1

Overview

Sixteen concepts in one continuous build: tell an expression from an equation, define what makes an equation linear in one variable, solve by balancing and its shortcut (transposing), extend to a variable on both sides, then reduce a messier equation – fractions, brackets – to that same simple form. Every row below traces to the chapter's own worked examples: 2x − 3 = x + 2, and the fraction-clearing Examples 16 and 17.

Section 3

What Makes an Equation Linear

KnowledgeSkillAbilityLevel
A linear equation in one variable has exactly one variable, at degree 1Test a given equation against both rulesClassificationBasic
A coefficient beside a letter (5z) is not the same as an exponent on it (z⁵)Distinguish a coefficient from a power by its notationDiscriminationIntermediate
Recommended strategy – activity-based teachingCircle the distinct letters, not the terms, in a sample equation so repeated variables aren't miscounted.
Where this shows upA costing model with a fixed charge plus a rate per unit made is a linear equation in one variable, the unit count being the unknown.
Section 4

Balancing and Transposing

KnowledgeSkillAbilityLevel
The same operation on both sides of an equation preserves equalitySolve a one-step equation by applying an operation to both sidesProcedural fluencyBasic
Transposing is the balancing step written as a shortcut: a term flips sign when it crosses the equals signTranspose a term correctly, explaining it as the balancing step in disguiseProcedural fluencyIntermediate
Checking means substituting the answer back into the original equation, not redoing the same workingVerify a solution by substitution into both original sidesVerification reasoningBasic
Recommended strategy – experiential teachingUse a real two-pan balance: lift a weight off only one pan and let students see the immediate, obvious tilt.
Where this shows upSharing a bill equally means the same adjustment to every share; equation-solving software rearranges terms by exactly the transposing rule.
Section 5

A Variable on Both Sides

KnowledgeSkillAbilityLevel
A variable term transposes exactly like a number termTranspose a variable term from one side of an equation to the otherProcedural fluencyIntermediate
An equation may have expressions with the variable on both sides, not just a bare number on oneSolve an equation with the variable on both sides, in fullProcedural fluencyIntermediate
Recommended strategy – worked-example teachingWalk through 2x − 3 = x + 2 one transposition at a time, naming the move at each step, before assigning independent practice.
Where this shows upComparing two mobile tariffs that each charge per minute puts the unknown minute count on both sides of the comparison.
