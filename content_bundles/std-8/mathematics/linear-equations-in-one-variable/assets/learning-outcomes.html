Learning Outcomes

Linear Equations in One Variable. 10 sections, matching the Teacher Training deck. No curriculum-coded outcome in the platform's table is specific to this chapter (see Section 8), so every outcome below is drawn directly from the chapter's own worked examples and concept data.

Section 1

By the End of This Chapter

Every outcome in this document is measurable and assessment-ready – a teacher can watch a student do each one and judge, without ambiguity, whether it has been met.

  • •Distinguish an expression from an equation, and identify the LHS and RHS of a given equation.
  • •Test whether a given equation is linear in one variable, using the one-variable and degree-1 rules.
  • •Solve a linear equation using the balance principle and its transposing shortcut.
  • •Solve a linear equation with the variable appearing on both sides.
  • •Clear fractions by multiplying every term by the LCM of the denominators, and open brackets fully before transposing.
  • •Translate a real-world problem (numbers, ages, perimeters, currency notes) into a linear equation and solve it.
Section 2

Expression vs. Equation

  • LOSort a mixed list of algebraic statements into expressions and equations, using the equals-sign test.
  • LOIdentify the LHS and RHS of any given equation, regardless of which side carries the bare number.
  • LOCheck whether a given value is a solution of an equation by substituting into both sides.
PrerequisitesBasic algebraic notation from earlier grades (terms, variables, coefficients).
Section 3

What Makes an Equation Linear

  • LOState the two conditions for an equation to be linear in one variable: exactly one variable, and highest power 1.
  • LODistinguish a coefficient beside a variable from an exponent on it.
Section 4

Balancing and Transposing

  • LOSolve a one-step or two-step linear equation by applying the same operation to both sides.
  • LOTranspose a term across the equals sign, correctly flipping its sign, and explain it as a balancing shortcut.
  • LOVerify a solution by substituting it into the original equation, as a genuinely independent check.
Section 5

A Variable on Both Sides

  • LOSolve a linear equation where the variable appears in expressions on both sides.
  • LOTranspose a variable term exactly as confidently as a numeric term.
Section 6

Reducing to Simpler Form

  • LOIdentify the LCM of the denominators in an equation and multiply every term by it to clear fractions.
  • LOFully expand a bracketed equation before transposing any term.
  • LOVerify a solved answer against both original, unsimplified sides of the equation.

Section 7

Where Linear Equations Are Used

  • LOTranslate a word problem about numbers, ages, perimeters or currency notes into a linear equation.
  • LOCompare two different choices of unknown for the same word problem and judge which produces a simpler equation.
Section 8

A Note on Curriculum Coverage

The platform's curriculum-outcome table lists 18 outcomes against this chapter's id – identically the same 18 attached to Chapter 1 (Rational Numbers) – covering divisibility rules, squares and cubes, algebraic identities, profit and loss, quadrilaterals, 3-D shapes and mensuration. None of the 18 specifically describe linear equations; the nearest in spirit, "solve puzzles and daily life problems using variables," is explicitly grouped in the source data under the Algebraic Expressions and Identities chapter's competency, alongside "multiply algebraic expressions" and "use algebraic identities," not under equation-solving. This looks like a subject-wide outcome set bulk-attached to every chapter rather than split correctly per chapter, so none of the 18 are cited in this document; every outcome above is drawn directly from the chapter's own content instead.

Section 10

Closing

This chapter's real spine is transposing – one technique, first justified by balancing, then applied unchanged whether the extra term is a number, a variable, or buried inside a fraction or a bracket. A student who can transpose confidently and always checks the final answer against both original sides has, in a concrete and checkable sense, finished the chapter.
