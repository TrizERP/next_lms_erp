Section 1

Overview

Each section below rubrics one part of the chapter across four bands – Excellent, Good, Fair, Needs Improvement – so a mark reflects procedural understanding, not just a correctly reached final value. Each section closes with sample questions new to this document.

Section 2

Expression vs. Equation

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
SortingSorts any mixed list correctly, explains the equals-sign test unpromptedSorts correctly with a reminder of the testSorts only the obvious casesCan't distinguish the two
Solution CheckingChecks any value by substituting into both sides, unpromptedChecks correctly when askedOnly substitutes into one sideDoesn't know what a solution means
Sample questions
  • MCQ (1 mark): Which of these is an expression, not an equation? (a) 7 = 2x − 1 (b) 3x + 5 (c) x = 4 (d) 2x = x + 6
  • Short Answer (2 marks): For the equation 3 = 5x − 2, identify the LHS and the RHS without rearranging.
Section 3

What Makes an Equation Linear

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
Linearity TestApplies both rules (one variable, degree 1) correctly to any equationApplies both rules with occasional slipsApplies only one of the two rulesCan't test either rule
Sample questions
  • MCQ (1 mark): Which equation is linear in one variable? (a) 3x + 2y = 5 (b) x³ = 27 (c) 7 − 4y = 3y + 1 (d) xy = 12
Section 4

Balancing and Transposing

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
BalancingApplies the same operation to both sides, every time, without being remindedApplies correctly after a reminderOperates on one side only, inconsistentlyDoesn't preserve the balance
TransposingTransposes any term correctly, explaining the sign flip as a balancing shortcutTransposes correctly, can't yet explain whyTransposes only simple numeric termsCan't transpose reliably
Sample questions
  • Short Answer (2 marks): Solve 9x − 3 = 15 using the balance principle, writing the operation applied to both sides at each step.
  • HOTS (3 marks): A student solves 2x + 5 = 11 by writing "2x = 11 + 5". Identify the error and explain the correct transposing rule.
Section 5

A Variable on Both Sides

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
Variable TranspositionTransposes a variable term exactly as confidently as a numeric termTransposes correctly with a reminder that x is a number tooTransposes numbers but hesitates over variable termsCan't solve equations with variables on both sides
Sample questions
  • Short Answer (2 marks): Solve 5x + 9 = 5 + 3x.
  • Numerical (3 marks): Solve 4(x − 3) = 2x + 6.
Section 6

Reducing to Simpler Form

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
Clearing FractionsMultiplies every term, whole numbers included, by the correct LCMMultiplies correctly with a reminder to include whole-number termsMultiplies only the fractional termsCan't identify or apply the LCM
Opening BracketsExpands fully before transposing, every timeExpands correctly with a reminderInterleaves expanding and transposing, with sign slipsCan't expand brackets reliably
Final CheckAlways verifies against both original (unsimplified) sidesVerifies when promptedRe-does the working instead of substitutingSkips checking entirely
Sample questions
  • Numerical (4 marks): Solve (3t − 2)/4 − (2t + 3)/3 = 2/3 − t, clearing fractions first.
  • Short Answer (3 marks): Solve 3(t − 3) = 5(2t + 1), opening both brackets before transposing.
Section 7

Where Linear Equations Are Used

CriterionExcellentGoodFairNeeds Improvement
Translation to an EquationChooses a sensible unknown and writes a correct equation unpromptedWrites a correct equation with guidance on the unknownWrites a partially correct equationCan't translate the word problem at all
Sample questions
  • Case Based (4 marks): A sum of ₹590 is made up of ₹50 and ₹10 notes, 19 notes in total. Form and solve an equation to find how many of each.
  • Case Based (4 marks): The perimeter of a rectangle is 13 cm more than twice its width. If the length is 7 cm, find the width by forming an equation.

Section 10

Putting It Together

Notes for teachers
  • Vocabulary across the chapter: expression, equation, LHS, RHS, solution, transpose, LCM, linear
  • Activities: expression/equation sorting, two-pan balance demonstration, step-by-step transposing walkthrough, dual-unknown word-problem comparison
  • Bloom's verbs used throughout: identify, solve, transpose, verify, translate, justify
  • Evidence types: sorted lists, step-by-step solutions, substitution checks, translated word problems
  • Question bank size: 9 questions in the Teacher Training deck's web slide, 9 more in its richer PDF slide, and 11 across this document's Sections 2–7 – nearly 30 real, chapter-grounded questions in total, none repeated across the three.
